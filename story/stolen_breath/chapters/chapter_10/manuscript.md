# Chapter 10 // Tsunari

> *Record closed. Boy, seven years old. Fast hands, good eyes. Family unknown. Taken for the draft. No belongings returned.*

***

I wake on my back. The bench is under me, the prowler already moving, and my wrists are in a mag-cuff on my chest.

The side of my head aches where the jamb caught it. The braid is empty. The hooks are gone from the hips, and the loss of that weight is the first honest fact in the cabin. The collar is still up. The goggles are not. Outside, the treads knock a steady ugly rhythm, and grit ticks the hull. I test the cuff. It holds. My fingers work. He left me that.

The cuff holds my wrists together in front of me. I get both hands under me and sit up. The cabin tips while I do it, or my head does. I stay sitting. A mistake, or a bargain he made with himself before I was awake to price it.

He is on the bench facing mine. There is barely a floor between us. Harness smoking faintly at the wing roots. Eyes too bright. The wing is folded tight because the cabin will not take it any other way, and his six feet four is folded with it, knee already on my side of the floor, close enough that I can feel the heat of it. Ozone and hot metal, the same smell as the brick against my cheek. I keep my eyes on his hands. One of them shakes. The other is flat on his knee, trying to be a soldier's hand, and losing.

The heat sits on my cheek the way it sat in the shack. In the shack it was the fight. Here the fight is over, and he is still burning. A shaking man. A closed hatch. He had the weight to finish me on that floor, and he put me in a cuff instead. I watch the hand that is losing.

His mouth is parted. He is breathing on a count, and the count is too short for a man at rest. I watch the count. The tiller is empty. The prowler is running the route it was given, and the route does not need a man. A closed hatch. He put me in the back and sat where he could see the door and the cuff.

The quiet is gone.

In the shack his skull had been a held breath. I had my hand on the metal and the drill cut out, and I did not know the trick. Here the breath is back, and it is hurting him. The tremor starts in the fingers and climbs the forearm. He sees me see it and hates that, which tells me the hate is not for me alone.

"Don't," he says.

"Don't what. Sit here while you cook?"

"Don't touch me."

"You're already cooking." The heat reaches the cuff. The metal is warm from him, not from the prowler. I stay sitting. His knee is already against my bench, so the reach is a lean, not a stand. Both wrists come up together. I put two fingers on the inside of his wrist, skin to skin, the way you check a pulse. The pulse is what I let it look like. I want to know if the shake stops.

The tremor stops.

His eyes shut. The smoke at the harness thins. Air goes into him and comes out like a man who has been allowed a room. When he looks at me again he is ashamed of the relief. The shame is how I know the relief is real. He still will not look at the place where my fingers meet his skin.

I take the fingers away.

The tremor returns meaner, as if the absence charged it. He makes a sound he does not mean to make, small, in the teeth. I put the fingers back. It stops. The cool of my skin takes the heat off his wrist. I was taught that part. The shake stopping is a second thing, and I do not have a name for it. A switch. On when I touch. Off when I don't. I hate how clean the test is. The sound he made is why I do not take the hand away again.

"Say what it did," I tell him.

"No."

"Your eyes closed."

"A malfunction closes nothing." His gaze hits my mouth and leaves, fast. He looked, and he did not want me to catch the look. I keep my face bored. Bored gets a better price.

"Bullshit."

He doesn't answer. The smoke at the harness thins to almost nothing. His pulse tries to be orderly under two fingers. Orderly is not calm. I can feel the difference, and I do not tell him I can.

"You need the hand," I say.

"I need the packet." His voice is wrecked. "The hand is a malfunction."

"Call it that on your report." I keep the fingers light, easy to lift. "If you had called the shack in, I would be on a table. This rig is still on its route. So we can sit here and pretend you are in charge, or you can tell me where it is taking us before the gate does."

He looks at the hatch. He looks at the cuff. He does not look at my fingers. The not-looking is the tell. The hand is the thing he cannot afford.

"The route is the gate," he says. "I have not changed it."

"Then change it."

"South. When I take the tiller."

"Because they'll open me."

"Because they'll open both of us, if anyone repeats what your hand just did."

"Who's at the tiller?"

"No one."

Useful. He is afraid of the same minute I am. A man who needs a touch he cannot explain will bargain. A man who is ashamed of the bargain will lie to his own side before he lies to me. The heat is still on my fingers. I can work with the bargain. I cannot work with a corpse, and if I take the hand away for long enough I think I get one.

"Then the hand stays," I say. "Until we're out of this box. You don't ask what I am."

"The cuff stays until the post," he says.

The city leaves a boy-shaped hole in people. The face across from me is the age that hole grows up to be. Fast hands. I watched them trap a hook. *Don't fill it in.* I will not do him the violence of a life I was not given.

The bodkin left the braid on the shack floor. If he brought it, it is in a pocket. My hand wants the weight. The belt is close enough to try. A grab ends the deal, and the deal is the only thing between my wrists and a table.

"Deal," he says, and the word costs him.

He does not thank me. Thanks would make the palm a kindness, and a kindness is harder to price than a need. The graveyard comes up as a dark shine, bowls holding nothing, the day's heat already gone out of them. I count the bowls because a count is a road. His pulse stays even under my hand. I ease the palm, a test, and the shake answers before I have lifted clear. He breathes in through his teeth.

"Leave it," he says.

"Say please and I'll think about it."

"Leave it."

The prowler hits a rut. The palm slips. He leans into the place where it was, fast, before I have decided to give it back. *There.* I set the hand down again and watch him hate the lean. Hate is a lever. Need is a better one.

I leave it. The bowls fall behind. His skin is too hot under the palm, and I am not moving the palm. The wing is folded along the bench behind him, too big for the room, and the heat off the roots sits on my knee. I could shift away. I don't. I have already heard the sound he makes when the contact breaks. I am not ready to hear it again.

A lamp from the road hits his face and leaves. Fever-bright eyes. The scar on the jaw, a pale hook of old burn. I look at the scar because it is a fact. The mouth is a man trying to get a full breath. The bowls run out. The road is still the gate, and my hand stays, and his pulse stays where I put it. For as long as the palm stays, he can drive a bargain. The moment I lift it, he is a patient. I know which one I can survive.
