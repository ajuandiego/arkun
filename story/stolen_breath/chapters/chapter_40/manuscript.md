# Chapter 40 // Tsunari

> *The stills fill at dawn, and the shelters open when the freeze drops. No house takes the lee for itself.*

***

Ren finds us at the edge of the south water.

He should be at the clinic with Drew's lie in his mouth. He is on a roof with a cloth over his face and the rattle hidden badly, a dry catch every few steps that the cloth does not cover. The market under him is stalls and pipe-steam and people who price a stranger before they price his story.

"Don't," I say.

"I'm the introduction," he says. "Then I leave. She won't talk to a wing she hasn't priced, and she won't price you if a boy with bad lungs walks you in like a parade."

Madame Vrena Chen keeps a stall that is a government with a canvas roof. Baseline. Gray at the temples. A grease-stained duster over industrial silk, coin-rings heavy on the fingers, a brass respirator pushed up on her forehead so she can use her own mouth. She looks at the sky once. She was alive before the copper.

"Stills at dawn," she says. "Shelters when the freeze drops. You want hours, parts, and a centrifuge that isn't a toy." Her eyes go to Vram and stay. "I remember the patrols, three winters back. A boy missed curfew. You broke the collarbone. Now you stand in my market with your hands showing and a page in her sling."

Vram stands where she can see the hands. He does not tuck the torn wing behind a story. "I flew those sweeps. I won't retell them. If you need a man to hate, use me and still sell her the hours. The lee over this market fails in fourteen months. Read the page before you decide we are a raid."

Chen reads. Her thumb stops on the line about the downwind wards. The market moves around us, a woman arguing a filter price, a child sent for a canister and scolded for running.

"Fourteen months," Chen says. "Then the afternoons here look like the wards you just pulled the spill off. My stills fill at dawn because the spill still reaches this roof. You want me to spend centrifuge hours on a page."

"I want the hours on the common clock," I say. "Not a private tank. He hauls at dawn. Filters, canisters, whatever your pipes will take. He does not break a joint to prove the wing still works."

"And the shelters."

"When the freeze drops. A bench. The same bench your own people use."

She looks at him again, at the hands, at the torn vane he has not hidden. "You haul what I point at. You stop when my fitter says the joint will take no more. You do not preach on my stair."

"Done," he says.

Ren shifts his weight toward useful. I put two fingers on his wrist, the old signal. Go home. He goes, this time, because Chen's people are watching and he is not stupid in a crowd. The rattle follows him into the pipe. I let it.

"No house takes the lee for itself," Chen says. She folds the page and gives it back. "He looked at me when I spoke. He did not look at the girls on the stair."

"We'll haul," I say.

"You'll haul at dawn." She taps the respirator back down, because the conversation is over. "And you'll tell the boy with the bad lungs that my roof is not a hallway."
