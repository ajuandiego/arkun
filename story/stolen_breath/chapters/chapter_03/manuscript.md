# Chapter 3 // Vram

> **The leash.** The socket at the back of the neck, and the signal the Spire sends through it. Hesitate, and it hurts. Leave it on for years, and it starts to cook the man.

***

The roost has no rail.

I stand on the lip, boots on frost. The mast starts in the center, in Sector 1, and does not stop until it has come up through the glass and set this cap of stone on the outside of the crown. Three thousand feet of it under the toes. The shield is a shallow vault of hexagonal ribs, running out and down to the wall. Inside, the light is emerald, and the towers stand in it, small, a city keeping its own day. Outside, the air is copper and violet, and the frost is already on the stone. 

Wind comes off the glass hard enough to push a man who is only a man. The cap is three hundred feet of stone and windbreak, open the whole way around, and the lip still feels narrow. Above the mast, one of the observers holds its place. A black hull the size of a block, ever watching, hovering over the dome since I can remember. The wind is busy and the hull does not take any of it. 

The socket at the back of my neck flares. *Here we go again.* 

I give the ship one glance and take the glance back. *Bastards.* They are the ones who put this leash on my neck. A round mouth of metal where the head meets the spine. A thin drill turns in the bone, bored, working a nerve it has owned for years. I set my teeth and keep my hands off it. The Spire sends it. Rubbing has never made the Spire kinder.

The Resource Wars took the fuel, the refineries, and every part a plane needed in order to fly. They grew the wing on us instead. We take the hit, then we heal. They feed us tasteless paste up here and we can't complain about it. We can't complain about anything.

Under the mast the ring is lamps and zinc. Sector 09 burns small. The south gate is a red cluster, and a prowler has been sitting in it since the drill started. Past the gate, Pylon Seven stands in the barrens, a tower with its foot in the ground and violet up the shaft. The copper is thicker over it. The truck has not moved. The drill has not stopped. I watch both.

"Hold still, Commander," Rook says. "You're toasting my flatbread."

Ferrin Calder is on an ammo crate under the windbreak, hawk wings half open to the stone, gray with dark bars across the vanes. The scar at the left of his mouth pulls when he grins. A cold round of flatbread sits on his glove. He leans it toward my shoulder as if the heat there is a stove he was issued and means to use before somebody writes the use down.

"Put that on my skin and you can retrieve it from the street," I say.

"It's pressed algae." He eats the ration strip in his other hand instead, and makes a face he wants me to see. "Cold, it still tastes like the tank. On you it almost passes for food. Walking furnace on the lip and they won't let a man use it. Waste of a good burn."

I groan. *Jerk.* Ok, it is kind of funny.

"South stacks were filthy yesterday, so I stalled the patrol. Write me up, or admit the air over Sector 09 is garbage and I did the wing a favor."

"Check your pins before you blame the air." The prowler's lamps have not changed since I took the lip. "The hour started when that truck stopped. We hold until it leaves, or until the leash spends us. I am not throwing a wing at a parked truck."

Cassian does not laugh. His arms stay folded, his wings tight, a hawk's wing, made to keep a lane and come home in it. "Then say the hold out loud before the north side strips a vane to decorate the point. The rigging over there is screaming. Launch into that for nothing and we own the repair, and the empty sky, and your mood."

"We preen after."

"Speak for the raven." Rook flicks a primary and the grin widens on the scar. "You grow the feather back before the cursing is finished. Mine whistle for weeks. Toby has to listen to it, which is a cruelty even for you."

"I can hear the feather speech from the rack."

Toby Vance comes off the colonnade with a foil patch in both hands and a quartz bird the size of a thumb pinched against one palm. He sees that he is still holding the bird, and he puts it into the pouch the way a man puts away a thing he does not want stepped on. Cream vanes, rust at the tips. He twitches when the mast hums in the stone. Cassian's brother. The youngest body on a roost that spends the young ones first.

"You're squinting," he says. "Left side of the jaw. The hour is sitting in it. I kept the ice."

"You need the ice."

"I ate." He holds the patch out. His eyes get as far as my chin. "Please, sir."

I take it. The gel is cold on the red skin around the socket, and the drill drops from a scream to a wire. The foot of the wall is gone behind the curve. Toby's shoulders drop with the wire.

The cold stays on the skin. The wire is still in the bone, only smaller, and I know which of the two will be here when the gel gives up. I leave the patch where he set it, and I leave the thanks unsaid. He would spend his own ice on the strength of a kind word, and the next hour is coming whether the truck moves or not.

"Better," I say.

"Good." A smile starts. He puts it away when he sees that I caught it. "It will climb again."

"I know."

Amari has the south corner and has not looked at the bread once. Blonde hair cut for the hood, a few strands at the jaw, wheat wings darker along the leading edge. The wind moves her vanes and does not move the rest of her.

"South face is dirty," she says. "It comes off the gate, off that prowler, and a lazy beat dumps you in the sand." She looks at me. "Wait for a clean lane and whatever they are about to ring for will already be in the mirrors."

The siren hits before anyone answers her.

Three wails take the cap, close enough that the buzz sits in the teeth. The crown lights snap red. The socket answers with a snap into the bone, and I go to a knee because the leash does not ask. The pain whites out the cap. *Fuck.* The order comes through it. *breach, south wall, a jack in a service door. catch alive. subject wanted for examination.*

Rook's bread hits the stone. His hand drops to the dagger on his thigh. Cassian's wings crack once for balance and fold tight. Toby's empty hand stays out, the patch already cold on my neck. Amari's vanes jump and settle. Same snap. The leash takes a broadcast, and this one went to every socket on the cap.

I am on my feet before the white clears. Staying on the knee costs more than the drop will. The playback comes up with the stitch: a heat smear under the south bridge, low and already moving. The smear is all the glass will give me.

"South wall," I say. "Catch alive. Subject wanted for examination. Cassian, hold the roost until I call the lane. Rook, Toby, lights on the bridge. If the runner breaks for the roofs, pin the roofs. You do not drop into the street for a ghost."

"A ghost," Cassian says. "You gave it the roofs off a smear."

"Nothing on the leash. If it clears the bridge, the mirrors take the dive. I am not waiting for a clean lane."

The scar sits in a straight mouth. Rook's voice has no joke left in it. "Alive. For examination."

"That's the order." The stitch flares for the time I spent on the sentence. I roll my neck and the pain sits down, still there, finished with the argument. "Amari."

"South face. Still dirty to the gate. The prowler hasn't moved."

"Clear the lip."

Toby steps back under the windbreak. His eyes stay on the socket, as if the patch might peel in the wind and he means to see it happen. Rook lifts two fingers, short of a salute. Cassian watches the air under the lip, wings still tight, already inside the launch he hopes I will not call. Amari stays on the corner and gives me the angle with her chin.

I open the wings. Fourteen feet of black vane. The beat puts a blue-violet sheen on the shafts, there and gone. Cold slaps the live skin where the feathers leave the back, and then the heat eats the slap. I lean.

I have done this since they threw me off a lower ledge as a boy and told a number to fly. The stomach goes, the boots find nothing, and the wings take the third second. The sergeant had no name I was allowed to keep. The air kept me then, and it is the same air.

The mast falls away. The wing tucks. For three breaths there is only the drop, the wall a gray streak, the red lights racing up past my boots, and the whistle starting in the feathers. Then the wings bite, the air hits like a door, and I level over the ring and take the south face she has been watching.

The gate swells. The prowler is a pale block in its own lamps. Copper on the span. No shoulders in it.

I do not give the runner the time.

The wind is dirty. I beat, and the sand comes up.
