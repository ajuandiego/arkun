# Chapter 6 // Vram

> *Catch the runner alive. Subject wanted for examination.*

***

The glass wind shoves grit through the rows, and I bank above it instead of going down.

The order is already in the socket. Catch the runner alive. Subject wanted for examination. The file comes with the runner. I could break that order in one stoop, the slate in my hand before the suits finish shouting. I do not take it. The suits can have the first minute. I want to see what it does with the minute.

From up here a runner is usually a bright streak against sand that has already given up its heat. This one stays cool. The smear takes a shape between the bowls. Narrow through the shoulder, a hook. The runner keeps to the bowls that held the afternoon, the same temperature as the glass. I stay up and come around again. I want to get a closer look.

The first suit's lamp dies in the trench. A pop, small enough that the wind almost keeps it. He is down in the sand, swearing up at the one who stayed out of it. The runner leaves the bowl in one push and is on his shoulder. Higher than the suit. Then off him. From up here that is the leg, and no hands. Left him breathing.

The second does not go into the trench. His rifle lifts. A bowl comes apart in black splinters, and I lose the runner against the glass that is still holding the day. Three breaths. When the dust moves, he is on the berm and the runner is already past him. The dust is still in the air. No second blow I can see. A tied man would have cost a minute, and the minute gets spent on me.

Clean. I hate how well the word fits. No panic, and no flash for the berm to aim by. I have had corporals with years in the legion do worse where I could see the hands. The stitch behind the eye agrees with the hate and not with the pleasure. I stay up anyway. The order wants the slate, and the hands that have it. I want the next move before I take either.

"One is down," the berm says, thick, into a speaker. "She is armed. She is small and she hits like the ground moved."

Small is the wrong word. *She?* There is no second body in the row.

She left the glass and put herself on an armored man. One push out of the leg, higher than his shoulder, and she was already off him. *Not human.* I have that much. No wing on her, so she is not Avian. A Lycan keeps a heat I could have read off the glass. A Ceraton keeps the size. A Galvan keeps a field, a prickle when they spend it. She kept none of them. Cool, narrow, and the leg. *Unless.* Wait, is this possible? *She might be... a Raptor.*

On the next pass I drop lower than I need to, hunting the leg that just put her on that suit. She lets me have the pass and uses it. One look at the wing where it leaves the back, one at the hip, one at the distance a dive would need if she stayed in that lane. She is not admiring the bird. She is building the box she means to put me in. I have had enemies shoot at the chest because the chest is obvious. She is pricing the reach. That is the kind of attention I was trained to kill, and I am up here letting it finish its count.

I make the next pass shorter. If she is counting, and she is counting, the shorter pass tells her I am about to come down. I want to know whether she believes the tell. She waits under a bowl until the pressure of me hits, then she moves while I am committed to a line I already chose. When I come around, the lane I marked is empty. Two rows deeper. She used my own pass as cover.

*Good.*

The leash flares at the thought. Admiration is not an order. I roll the neck and the pain sits down, unfinished, and I am still pleased. The anger I owe a wasted stoop does not come. What comes is the surprise, and the stitch for being surprised.

The lane bends. A tilted bowl sits where my line wants to be. She is already on the far side of it. A sheet of fallen mirror chokes the near gap, not enough to close the lane, enough to make the lane look stupid. One gap stays open at the bend, the vain one, the one a wing takes if the wing believes she is still standing in it.

She watched the passes. She put the glass in my mouth and left me a gift I am meant to be vain enough to take.

I see the rest of it in a shard by her boot, the angle of the wing caught in the black scrap, and her eyes on the scrap instead of on the sky. She is not hoping. She has the line. If I commit to the bowl, the curve takes the wing and she is gone down the far side. If I take the vain gap, I am through and she is already off the spot I aimed at. She mapped the stoop before I spent it. The scouts were the exercise. I am the problem she saved the minute for.

"Clever girl." The thought escaped out loud against my will.

The leash hates the tone. A hot stitch, small, a reminder that respect is not retrieve. *Fuck.* I take the gap anyway, because the gap is real and because I want the angle she thought she had finished. The wings tuck. Grit and black splinters. The whistle starts in the feathers. I go through on the side she did not quite close.

She hears it. A runner with any sense would spend the open lane and pray the dust does the rest. She turns into the dive with the hook already up, fast and low, the strike aimed at the place a neck would be if I were fool enough to lead with it. They run from the wing. She comes at the beak, and I am glad, which the stitch treats as a crime. I have time to feel both. A killing stoop would have put her through the bowl and saved me the argument. I want the hands, and the slate in them, and the woman who counted my passes still alive enough to know the gap failed her.

I hit her anyway.

The arm takes her at the waist as the rotted door of the mirror shack gives up. Wood, dust, a hard sound out of her that comes back armed. We go through together. The floor is sand and wire and broken mirror, and the first fall is mine, which means the second one will be the one she uses. The hook is still in her hand. My forearm is already across it. The socket screams from the impact, skin hot, metal indifferent, and I do not give her the nape.

Cool, where my arm locked. The wind outside is a furnace full of acid dust. She is not. The collar has come off the nape. Skin, where a socket would sit. *Uncollared.* The leg already said it. I do not have a free second to spend on the surprise.

She gets a knee under her. The door behind us is a mouth full of grit and the howl. The interior is low, too low for the wing to be a wing. She knows that. She picked it. I can see the measure happening behind the goggles, amber glass, the pupils cut to slits. *Welder's glass. Or a slit that is actually hers.* The same calculation she spent on the bowls.

"You can run for the back," I say. "The ceiling will not save you twice."

She looks at the hook, at the wing jammed crooked against the jamb, at the gap she built and I spent. No plea in it. The corner of her mouth moves, not a smile, the face of a person who has been scored on and is already rewriting the count.

The stitch wants her opened on this floor. My hands are busy keeping the beak out of my neck, and the part of me that stayed in the air is still glad she made me work for the door.

She comes up off the sand with the second hook, and the room gets small.
