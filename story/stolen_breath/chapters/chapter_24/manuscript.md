# Chapter 24 // Tsunari

> *The dust gets into the seals, the teeth, the lungs. When the sun goes, the cold bites harder than the dogs. If you leave the dome, don't look up. The sky out here is waiting.*

***

The air is a fist.

I have been on roofs. I have dropped off a bridge and let the hooks eat a flange. I have never been held up by a wing. The harness keeps my thighs locked at his hips, and the wing does the rest, a beat so hard the strap jumps against the split in my shoulder. I keep the arm where the strap put it.

Flak opens in the chemical haze, soft ugly flowers that are not soft when the grit arrives. It hits my teeth. I taste the band, metal and something burned, and I do not have a hand free to cover my mouth. Both arms are around his neck. The hooks hang quiet on their loops. One wrong swing and a beak finds the socket, and the socket is the thing keeping his head clear enough to fly.

"Don't look up," I tell myself. That is road advice, from men who died of looking at the wrong sky. I look.

The wall is behind us and smaller than it has any right to be. The sky out here has no hexes in it. An observer holds past the lamps, nothing under the hull, the wind leaving it where it is. Copper, then a tear of searchlight, then his primaries cutting the tear. Black. The beat throws us and the stomach goes with it. I am strapped to the body doing it.

"Yard," he says.

I see it. Containers on the lakebed, one ruin, close enough that the flight is a few miles and my stomach has already spent them. A lane between the boxes if he can still see lanes. Behind us the lamps of the squad jump when they beat. I do not count the wings. Counting them makes them a rescue, and he already told me not to count on the miss.

A slug snaps past his knee. The air of it tugs my calf. Rook, or a gun that means it. I don't ask. I lock my ankles harder and I keep my eyes open, because if the wing fails I want the ground named before it takes me.

"If you drop me," I say, "I will haunt the wing."

"Hold through the flare." His arm tightens across my back, under the bad shoulder, careful and not gentle. "Then we are down. Down is a problem I know."

Another flower opens, closer. The light paints his throat and the scar at the jaw. My mouth stays where it is. The yard grows. Grit ticks the containers. I breathe when he breathes, because his ribs are the only clock I have.

Under the grit, at the lip where the band becomes the ruin, something coughs like a motor waking. A white line starts to climb out of a turret I can see and he has not turned for yet.

"Hose," I say into his collar. "Left of the lane."

His arm tightens. The yard stops being a rumor and becomes boxes, and a black mouth between them, and the white line coming up to meet the wing.
