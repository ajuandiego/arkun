# Chapter 21 // Corvus

> *Both of them. Alive. We need to understand what happened.*

***

The lab keeps my face at thirty. I am eighty, and I know which hour the last treatment bought. I do not spend that hour on a mirror. The mirror is for the people who still need to be convinced I can outlast them.

The bench is mine. White stone, a tray of slides I have already read, a glass of water I will not drink because the night has a taste and I would rather keep it. The south window is a dark hex. Under it the ring is a brown smear and a few red lamps at the gate. Redoubt Station 14 is past the lamps, on a road I closed with a word an hour ago. Weather. The word has had an hour to sit.

A girl from the glove line comes in with a fresh coat over her arm and stops when she sees I am still here.

"Director. They said the long half was over."

"For them." I do not look up from the slide. The stain on it is ordinary. A fever that stayed a fever. "Who is on the roost desk?"

"Lieutenant Vance. He gave the weather again after supper. He has not gone down."

"Leave the coat. Tell the desk I will call. And tell them a secretary does not spend my weapon."

She goes. The door shuts on the smell of the wash they use in the hall, sweet and thin. I am alone with the hour I bought, and the hour is already shorter than it was at dusk.

The ceiling array opens without a knock. No body. No sleeve. A voice, patient, the way a thing is patient when it does not have to stand in the room it is using. The glass of the window fogs at the edges, though nothing in here breathed but me.

"Your soldier's return dropped," it says. "The noise left him and came back. A woman was on the contact. She is the anomaly. Priority zero. Both of them. Alive. We need to understand what happened."

I set the slide down so my hand has a place to be that is not a fist.

"A name for the anomaly."

"No."

"Where she was made."

"No."

"A mechanism. What she did to the return. How long the quiet held. Whether it will hold again if I put another hand on him."

"No." The word does not rise. "Capture. Do not open her on the road. A corpse cannot be read, and a commander cooked in a ditch is a story you already tried to tell me. I disliked it."

"I told you weather."

"You told us a dead runner and a closed road. The return says the runner is on his skin. Bring both. Readable. The understanding is not your work."

The array goes quiet and does not close. It is waiting for me to be staff in my own lab. The anger arrives clean. I can use clean. Under it the vanity, which is older and less useful: he is the weapon I kept, the wing I would not let a committee dull, and a girl off the sand put a hand on him and the return obeyed her. Under the vanity I am tired. The cohort I will spend is young. The leash will climb in them until someone bleeds or comes home. I will sign that. The alternative is to be the director who lost the masterwork and called it weather.

"Alive," I say. "Both. I will send the wing he left, and a retrieval behind it. If he bleeds them, the gain stays up until he comes home or the wing fails. He will understand the price."

"Do not diagnose him over the beam."

"I don't have a diagnosis," I say. The truth of it sits on my tongue like a metal I was not issued. "I have an order."

The voice leaves. The fog on the window thins. The lab is white again, and mine, and smaller for having been borrowed.

I call the roost myself. The desk answers on the first tone, a young voice trying to sound like a watch.

"Director."

"Lieutenant Vance. Now."

A breath, a click, and Cassian is there. I can hear the ledge in him, the wind he has not stepped out of.

"Director. The south road is still closed. He has not called the clear."

"He will not. Weather is over. South post. Both of them are alive. The runner and your commander. You will bring them in readable. A retrieval is behind you. If he draws on the wing, you do not soften the gain to save a formation. The gain stays up until he comes home or the wing fails."

He is quiet long enough that I hear him choose which question he is allowed to ask.

"Readable," he says. "A live capture. Not a body."

"A body is a story I have already refused. Do not open her on the road. Do not cook him in a ditch and call it loyalty. And you do not get a reason you can fly with. Your brother gets less than you. If Tobin asks about the neck, the neck was the usual burn. If he asks to be in the flight, he is not, unless you have already decided to spend him."

"Understood."

"Say it so the log can keep it."

"South post. Both alive. Bring them in readable. Weather is over. The gain holds if he bleeds us."

"Good." I look at the dark hex and the red lamps under it. The hour in my face is thirty. The hour in my hands is the one I have left, and I am spending it on a man who chose a girl over the report I taught him to make. "Go get my weapon. Leave the doubt on the ledge."

The channel drops. I pull the wing list and I write the order under my own name, because a voice in the ceiling does not get to spend them without my hand on the page. Cassian. Amari on the wind, because she already called the south face dirty and she will not lie to make a lane. Rook, because a joke is not a refusal and he will fly mean if I tell him the supper is over. Tobin stays on the ledge unless his brother spends him. Behind them, a retrieval that does not love him. The gain, if he draws. I sign it. The ink is ordinary black.

I do not sit. Sitting is how a director becomes a woman waiting for a boy to do the hard part. I take the coat the girl left, and I do not put it on. The slides can wait until morning. The wing cannot.

On the way out I pass the long window of the culture rooms. One bench still has a glove on it. Someone flagged a number tonight and went home. The number is already on the road, wearing my soldiers, and I am the one who sent them.
