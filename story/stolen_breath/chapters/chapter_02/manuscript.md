# Chapter 2 // Tsunari

> **The service door.** A keypad off the south gate, on the wall's own wire. The wire stays open while the jack is seated.

***

Copper grit hangs thick under the bridge. I come along low in it. At the wall end I jump. The height is the legs, more than a body gets, and the hooks bite as I pull onto the landing. The door is a plain slab with the keypad at my chest. The wall is cold where my shoulder touches it, and the collar of my tunic is stuck to my throat. Across the sand the clinic lane is still buying the last of the day. Mash at one post, bread at the next, people moving because the night will freeze. This door is the dark between those lights.

"Last boot was the long pass," Ren says in my ear. His breath catches on the last word, the dry sound he pretends is only the climb. "Seven point eight from then. I'm on the roof that sees the corner. Drew stayed at the bench."

"Copy." I work the bodkin out of the braid. A twist, and the blade comes off in my glove. The tang under it is dark, cut for a jack Drew measured off a dead keypad. "T.J."

"Here." He is young and close to the mic, and he never is anywhere else. "I have the door's wire. You seat it, I get the picture. You hold still and the copy stays in the slate. Silt's thick, and the gate paint is on their own truck."

"Then count."

Ren's next breath scrapes. He swallows it, and I let him. A lecture on the sound in his chest will not add a second to the gap.

The jack is a recess under the keypad, the size of a thumb, silt gritted into the lip. I blow it clear, seat the tang, and the click runs up my wrist into the bone. The short lead from the slate on my chest clips to the pommel-end, and the tell goes blue. The Spire listens for a voice in the air. This session goes down the door's own wire, into the tap he already owns, and the closed copy sits against my ribs.

"Picture's up," T.J. says. "Doors, cameras, the pass list. I'm in their security net. Don't pull."

"Talk."

"South stair changes in six minutes. Two on the gate mouth, both facing the truck. This door isn't on their board. The sand is bare, and this bridge is the only crossing on the face. The camera over you is pointed at the joint, not the keypad. Hold. Let me see what I can find."

I hold. My free hand stays on the hook at my hip. The slate warms, a small heat over the bone.

"Memos," T.J. says. "Shift notes. A filter request on level two. Useless. An asset list, dome inventory, crates and serials. Another list, same shape. Nothing in them we can sell."

"Leave them."

"This one's different." His mouth is closer to the mic. "A staff list, sent to an observer. The dome keeping them current on who works the benches. Let me see what opens."

Ren's breath fills the ear and stays there. "Corner's coming back."

"Count," I say. The hook creaks in my glove. The stair has six minutes. The pass is the longer number. Both are getting shorter while he opens it.

"The first part reads," T.J. says. "Culture Lab 4. Active staff. There's a Thorne. Junior synthetics. S..."

"Stop."

The rest of the name finishes in my head and stays there. *Sora.* The death rolls listed her as dead and burned. I have lived ten years on a dead girl, and now there is a Thorne at a desk inside the wall, junior, doing living work. She would be twenty. It can't be.

The name pulls the memory of our old workshop in between the keypad and my glove. Sora under the lamp, too close to her face, tongue between her teeth, finishing the braid at the back of my skull. A copper scrap on the bench cuts the skin off her thumb. She holds the thumb up, proud of the bead. The tug of that braid is on me now. *Damn it.* I remember the thumb. I can't picture the face that would be twenty now.

"You don't put the rest on this set."

Silt ticks on the keypad. Ren's breath is there, and then it isn't, because he's holding it.

"We have the doors," T.J. says. "The rest of it won't sell."

"Then we're done," Ren says.

"It's in the slate," T.J. says. "You can pull."

"Pulling."

"Wait." His voice changes. Not the pass. Something he did not ask the door for. "More of it just sat on the session. I don't know this format. The door doesn't use this header."

"Read what opened."

"Outpost update," T.J. says. "Atmosphere reconditioning, complete in fourteen months. Sector 09, effect immediate. Sectors 01 through 08, sealed. Evacuation allocation, zero. Command, confirm elim." He stops. "It ciphers from there. I don't know why the first part opened."

The rest I can already see. Fourteen months and the air on the stair I climbed this morning turns into the hard hours, cloth all day, nights you do not sleep on the ground floor. Mercer keeps boiling water for people who will not have a lane. Ren's catch stops being a thing we pretend is the climb. The pylons south of the ring make that air, towers with their feet in the barrens, worse in their shadow. Push that over the wall and the street under my boots stops being a place people live.

"Say the first line again."

"Fourteen months. Sector 09 takes it. The inner sectors seal. Then confirm elim... And it ciphers."

"We came for intel," Ren says.

"I know," T.J. says. "I don't know what this is. It's in the slate with the rest. Tsune, the pass is at the corner. Pull."

I pull the lead, twist the blade back onto the tang, and seat the bodkin through the braid until the steel sits cold against my skull. The slate stays on the chest strap, face in, tell dark.

The keypad screams.

A klaxon, close enough to put a buzz in the fillings. The jack locks shut.

"Signal left the door," T.J. says. His voice goes thin and fast. "Twenty-five seconds before it has a place. Not on you yet. Move."

"I'll meet you at the stair," Ren says.

"You will not." The wall on my left is a hundred meters of smooth metal, no handhold the eye can love. "Stay on."

I drop off the landing. The hooks catch the lip and let go, the boots hit the sand under the span, and the knees take it the way they have since I was seventeen, the landing eaten, the shock thrown back into the metal. The silt takes the outline, and I am already moving.

Searchlights rake the main gate, white, then the red off the wall, then white again. The metal under my glove is cold enough to ache, and seamless. In the lane, shutters slam. A whistle cuts the first alley. A woman pulls a child by the wrist, and neither of them looks up.

The header sits in the ear beside the name I would not let him finish. The slate knocks my ribs with both of them, and I do not have a free hand for either.

"Stay low," T.J. says. "The light is still on the main gate. They're more interested in their own door than in yours. That lasts about as long as the log."

"Then I won't be on the stair."

"Prowler's nose is on the gate," T.J. says. "It hasn't committed. I have the bearing if it turns."

"Ren, where are you."

"Two roofs off the clinic stair. I can see the lights. Give me the time and I'm there."

A shadow crosses the searchlights, a wing tucked and falling out of the dark above the wall. Hawks I know by the narrow cut, a patrol that stays in its lane and goes home. A raven is the drop they order when they want someone opened, and the wing is too heavy for a lane. I try to put this shadow into one of those and it will not go. The feathers whistle, a sound with a body in it, and the heat of the dive reaches me before the shape clears the lamp, ozone and hot metal, the hair on my arm lifting as my hand finds the hook. *Fuck.*

"Ren. Get to Drew. Kill the set."

"Tsune, don't."

I cut the link.

The channel dies in the ear. The wing keeps coming. I run south, low, and I do not look up again until the roofs close over me.
