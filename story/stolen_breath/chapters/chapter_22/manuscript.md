# Chapter 22 // Tsunari

> *Feather to feather, bone to bone*
> *None of the Aerie falls alone.*
> *Break the wind and ride the squall*
> *If one must dive, we catch the fall.*

***

The wolf is on the concrete with one hand on the cut strap. The woman is down beside the pump, chest open where the plate stops, breathing like the charge cost her the next one. Up on the pad, the man is still short of the door. Alive. Government. The blood on my shoulder has stuck the tunic to the split, and the hooks are in my hands because I never put them back.

"Off this floor," I say. "You said the next prowler learns how."

"The next one is already learning." He is at the hatch, listening up. The post's set has gone from a tick to a voice, and the voice is not asking for weather. "They want us readable. That means alive, and it means they are close enough to say it out loud."

I step over the woman's boot. She does not grab. Her eyes find me and do not hold. Poor light, or the charge spent her. I do not spend a second look to decide which. The ring of the hatch is above us. He goes up first because the wing has to fold to fit, and I come after him with the bad shoulder in, hooks low, so the steel does not talk to the ladder.

The pad is lamps and grit. The man against the wall turns his head when the door opens. He gets a hand under himself. Vram puts a boot on the wrist and the hand stays down.

"Stay," he says, in the voice he used when he was still their commander. "You already had the door."

The man stays. A mouth on a pad will tell the next wing which way we stepped.

South of the lip the ground stops being a road and becomes the band: dark, a chemical sheen, the air wrong in the teeth. Containers sit on the far side of it like a rumor of a yard. Between here and there is open. He is already pulling a spare harness off the rack by the mast, the one that rides a passenger, not a wing.

"I can run the band," I say.

"You can run until the first lamp finds you. Then you are a target with a bad shoulder and I am a fool who let you." He buckles the harness across his own chest. The straps are made for a body that does not have a split under the cloth. He sees the blood and his jaw sets, and he routes the lower strap under the hurt instead of across it. "Hold. Thighs lock or you drag. Eyes shut when the lamps hit. If you let go, the band gets you, and I do not get a second grab."

"I don't shut my eyes for men who give orders."

"Shut them for the glare."

He pulls me in. My thighs lock at his hips or the harness is a story I fall out of. The plate of his chest is heat through the cloth, and the split shoulder goes stupid against it. I put my arms around his neck because the concrete is the other choice. The hooks hang from my wrists on their loops. One beak ticks his collar and I turn it out. The socket is under the cloth. I keep the steel off it.

Searchlights pin the post. White, hard, the kind that makes a face into a target. I shut my eyes. A voice on his old channel starts a cadence I do not know. I do not take the words. He cuts the channel with his jaw against my hair.

"They'll fire," I say.

"Some of them will miss on purpose. Do not count on the kindness. Count on the band being worse than the miss."

His arm locks behind my back. Every shift of the strap moves us against each other. My click sits in my throat while I count the shoulder, the hooks, his heartbeat against my ribs, the lip under his boots.

"Breathe," he says.

"I am."

He steps off.

The stomach goes, the boots find nothing, and the wing takes the air. The ground becomes the band, dark and shining, chemical in the teeth even up here. I am locked to a soldier I fought in a shack tonight. The harness creaks. His arms stay locked. I hold.

A flare opens behind us. I look once. Lamps, and bodies in them, a line of wings off the post. Then I stop measuring. The yard is still a rumor ahead. His mouth is at my hair, and the order he does not repeat is the one I am already doing.

My weight is in his harness. I do not let go.
