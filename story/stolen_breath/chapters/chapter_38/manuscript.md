# Chapter 38 // Vram

> *Iron that fears the hammer stays slag. Flesh that fears the fire stays mortal. Bow your neck to the anvil, or be swept out with the waste.*

***

They escort us because killing us in a pipe would waste the audience.

Two of them ahead, one behind, none of them the girl I left on her knees. The pipe opens onto a catwalk, and the catwalk opens onto water. Tsunari walks with the bad shoulder toward me, hooks low, counting the same things I am counting: the rail, the winch, the dark mouth at the left where a sluice drops. She does not kneel when the rows do. I do not tell her to.

The Grand Cistern holds its own weather. Water in the basin, iron catwalks, thousands of knees in water that comes to the shin. Breath and wet cloth. A man in the second row has his eyes shut and his mouth moving on a name that is not mine. A woman beside him has a child's wrap folded in her lap, empty.

The Forger stands where a voice carries without rising. Malakar stands at her shoulder. The rail-flail across his back gives one pneumatic hiss, a spring remembering itself, and then it is quiet. Her chest is bare above the brands. I look at her eyes instead. Flat.

Caelia is in the front row, shining and wrecked, cheeks still marked from the tunnel. She watches my hands as if they might still open toward her.

"Deliverer," the Forger says. The same flat voice, life-size. "The dome took your name and your wing and gave you a leash. I will give you an army."

The rows answer that word with a sound that is almost relief. The man with his eyes shut opens them.

"She is the right hand." A flick at Caelia, who straightens as if the flick were a crown. "The cold one is the price. We cut her here, and the flame stays lit. You have seen what the cup shows. You will not make these people watch the light go out."

Tsunari's mouth still has the cut. She is looking at the sluice winch, not at the cup, not at the girl. The army is a real offer. The girl is a real girl. I spit the sentence so the cistern can keep it.

"She is not my bride, and I am no one's god."

The kneelers make a sound like a wound. The man in the second row closes his hand on the empty wrap. Malakar's flail shifts on his back. The Forger's face does not.

"Then you are slag," she says. "And slag gets swept."

"Sweep," I say.

Caelia stares at me. The joy from the tunnel is gone. Malakar roars. The flail needs room, and the catwalks have room, and the spring on his back is already waking. Tsunari is moving before the roar finishes, toward the sluice.
