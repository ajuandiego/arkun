# Chapter 32 // Tsunari

***

The basement is under the yard, and the cold comes up through the stone like a second weather.

Kira put us here and went up to lie to her own road. The cot is narrow. A blanket that has been a curtain. His fever is a stove in a room that wants to freeze a sleeper, and the torn wing will not settle. It twitches and slaps the wall. If I stay on the floor he cooks on the side toward the ceiling and freezes on the side toward the stone. My skin takes a heat like this. It fixes nothing. It can drop an edge off a number, and the number is what will stand him up and drop him if I leave it.

I get on the cot. I wrap around him, chest to the burn of his ribs, the good arm over the shoulder that is not torn, the bad shoulder up where nothing can find the split. His breath catches, then lengthens. The wing tries to cover us and mostly fails. What it manages is ruined feather between our backs and the stair.

"Stay," he says. The fever is still in the word.

"I'm here."

He wakes in pieces. The yard first, then the boy, then the fact of my wrist under his hand.

"You should have gone with him."

"The boy went. Drew has the page. They will tell Mercer the mirrors ate the signal. I stayed. Those are different sentences."

"The dogs."

"Scattered in the aisle. If they come back down the stair, you stay on this cot." I put my palm on his chest and count. Fast, hot, not climbing for this minute. "Kira sold us a door she does not open herself. Boran still decides. That is the morning. This is the cot."

His hand finds my waist and stops. He already stopped once, in the culvert, forehead on my stomach, the heat still in him. My mouth is close to his jaw. I leave it there.

When his eyes clear, they are his. Gold, tired.

He looks at my mouth, at the blood drying on the sleeve, at the one cot and the wing that cannot fly. I keep my hand on his chest before he can make the next minute pretty.

"Partners, or nothing."

He is quiet long enough that I think the fever took the words. The coal of him kicks under my palm. Then his hand covers mine.

"Say the nothing," he says.

"No keeper. No collar with a nicer name. If you try to fly this wing at dawn, I know it is the fever and I will put you back down. If I run, you know it is a door, not a betrayal. We don't spend each other to feel safe."

"Partners," he says. "Or nothing. I heard you."

"Agreed is the word you owe me."

"Agreed."

He pulls me in under what is left of the wing. The cold stays on the far side of the feathers. His mouth touches my hair once, asking nothing. I stay awake. I count his pulse until it is slower, still hot, a number I can trust for an hour. Above the stair, Kira's boots cross once and do not come down. The yard ticks. I do not let the gray at the door be morning until the pulse stays where I put it.
