# Chapter 30 // Tsunari

***

The lead dog's nose is in the slit, and the slit is already a door.

I have the bodkin. The hooks are on the floor where I dropped them to drag him, one under my boot, one against the wall. Vram is on his side in the corner the wing makes, breath rasping, heat coming off him in a sheet the dog has already priced. If I open the door to swing, the aisle comes in with the dog. If I stay in the slit, I fight in a gap the width of my shoulders.

"Down," I tell him. "If you stand, the wing fills the box and I lose the corner."

He answers with a sound that might have been my name. The gold under his lids does not find me yet.

The rush is a shoulder and teeth, aimed at the place I am standing. I should be under it. I am beside it. The step does not take the time a step takes. The container stutters, a bad frame, the wall arriving twice. The dog hits the steel where I was. I am already at its shoulder, close enough to smell the meat on its breath and to see the eye roll for a target it has lost.

The bodkin goes in behind the blade of the shoulder. Deep enough to turn the charge. Hot blood on my wrist.

It snarls and rounds. The strange step is gone. I try for it and the second takes the time a second takes. I am only as fast as I was on the road, and the road was never fast enough for a mouth this close. The teeth close on air where my thigh was. I get the hook off the floor on the way to the door seam, and I put the beak into the gap the dog has to come through. It checks, bleeding, offended. My heart is too loud. The move is already spent. I am still in the container.

"Stay down," I tell Vram again.

He is trying to rise. The wing will not let him. It scrapes the container wall and fills the dark with the smell of burned feather. His eyes open, gold, fixed on the blood on my wrist.

"How many," he says.

"One in. More in the aisle. You do not spend the wing on a dog."

The lead one gathers on its haunches. Blood mats the shoulder. The yellow eye finds me again, surer, because I am where a body is supposed to be. I set my feet. The bodkin is slick in one hand and the hook is in the other, and I set the beak in the jamb where the next rush has to narrow. When it comes I meet it at the seam. Teeth take the boot leather and slip on the plate of the heel. I rake the same shoulder I already opened, and the dog yanks free rather than fold. Behind it, two more noses work the gap. They are waiting to see if the big one finishes.

I click once, the old hunting sound, small in the box. It comes back off the steel. None of them answer it.

The lead one shakes blood off its muzzle and sets itself a second time. Behind me the wing shifts, a heavy sound, a man getting his hands under him. The container is about to be too small.
