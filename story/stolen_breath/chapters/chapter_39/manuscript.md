# Chapter 39 // Tsunari

> **A rail-flail.** Iron on a spring, built to break a body that can fly. It needs room. A falling gate does not give it room.

***

The flail is a length of iron on a spring. It arms with one pneumatic hiss, and the catwalk gives it room. He swings at Vram's wing. The iron wants the span of a beat. The catwalk rings. I am already off the line of it, under the swing, because the wing is the target and the winch is not.

"Again," I say. "Make him spend the room."

Vram gives him the wing as a shape and not as a body, a half-beat that looks like a stumble and pulls the iron wide. The second swing takes the rail where his knee was. Sparks. The spring needs the length of the catwalk to be a weapon, and Malakar is happy to use the length. I get to the sluice winch while the iron is still out there. The last charge Drew packed for a door is in my palm. Acoustic, ugly, small. I set it where the gate's hinge complains, and I thumb it.

The gate above Malakar shudders, bolts screaming, and the iron falls into the room the flail wanted. He has a choice that lasts half a second: fold the weapon or lose the arm. He folds. The head of the flail slams its own spring and the spring howls. We run while the howl is still the loudest thing in the cistern. Behind us the rows are a roar of people who came for a cutting and got a fallen gate. I do not look for the girl in the front row. Looking is a second, and the second is the mouth of the pipe.

The aqueduct after that is a throat. We hit a bend and the wing jams, and the jam puts us chest to chest. My hooks are against his ribs. His breath is the same bad air as mine. His mouth is close enough that I can feel the heat of it on the cut in my lip. His hand stays on the wall beside my head, flat, the knuckles pale once and then eased. Mine stays on the slate strap.

"That was the last charge," I say.

"I know. I watched you spend it." His mouth does not close the inch. "The cut."

"Lip. Teeth are mine. Shoulder pulls. I can walk."

The exit is behind his shoulder, a darker piece of tunnel. Smoke is in it, and voices, the kind a market makes when it is close enough to smell the steam.

"Not here," I say.

"No." He is shaking with the fight and with the inch he is not crossing. "Partners. The word still stands."

"It stands. Move."

We move. Ahead, the dark goes toward the smoke. I keep the charge's aftertaste in my teeth, and the heat of his mouth on the cut.
