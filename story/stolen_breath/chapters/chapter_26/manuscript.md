# Chapter 26 // Tsunari

> *Water will not stop the burn. The clothes have to come off, and the skin has to be scraped. Shame is slower than the burn.*

***

The chemical keeps eating after we are out of it.

His shirt smokes at the hem. My tunic holds the burn against the split shoulder and the sleeve, and the wrap at my thigh has gone stiff and hot. Water off the ledge beads on the cloth and does nothing. I get the tunic off. The air on the burn is a mercy so sharp I make a sound. He is already pulling his own shirt, jaw locked, the ruined wing shifting when the cloth drags the root. Neither of us makes a speech about being bare. The burn is faster than the speech.

Cold in the culvert. His skin throws heat anyway, and the chemical dies where his arm crosses mine. I turn him. The root is a mess of scorched down and live quill, the flesh around it flinching before I touch it.

"Finish it," he says, before I ask.

I scrape with the side of my hand. Dust. Burned feather. I keep off the live quill until the dead stuff is on the stone, and then I have to come close to the place the wing leaves the back, because the chemical is packed in there and leaving it is how the burn keeps the root. He makes a sound he does not mean to make. His whole back tightens. The knuckles on the stone go white once. I stop my hand.

"That's the last of it," I say.

He turns anyway. Crowds me to the wall. Breath wrecked. Eyes gold. His mouth is already at my throat.

"Stop," he says. The word and the mouth arrive together. "Or don't. Tell me which."

The burn is off my skin. What is left is his hands on my hips, hot, shaking, staying where they are until I spend a word. I want the mouth. A silence here gets collected later by whoever needs a debt.

"The burn is off," I say. My voice is not steady. "The rest of this, you ask."

"I'm asking."

"Then yes."

He goes to his knees on the wet stone. The wing curves in, a wall against the dark of the pipe. He kisses the inside of my thigh where the chemical missed, and the heat of his mouth makes the cold of the culvert a fact I can set beside it. Then he is on me. Tongue, and the flat of it, and one hand spreading me while the other stays braced on the wall so the wing does not crush. I get my good hand in his hair. The bad shoulder stays on the stone and complains, and I let it complain.

"There," I say, when he finds it, and he stays.

I hear myself. His name is in it, the one he gave me at the post. My heel skids. He does not chase the skid. His fingers come in slow, and the shake starts in my stomach and goes out through the knee, and he keeps his mouth on the place that made it start. I come apart against him. The pipe takes the sound and gives it back smaller. He stays until the shake leaves my legs.

Then he pulls back before the rest. His forehead rests on my stomach. He is hard against his own thigh. He does not chase it. His hands have left my hips. They are flat on the stone.

"If I keep going I will burn you," he says. "The heat doesn't know the difference between a wing and a body. I do."

"Then we stop." I get my hand in his hair again, and I hold him where he put himself. "You stopped. Remember that you stopped."

We breathe. The clothes are a ruined pile that still smells like the hose. I do not put the tunic back on the split. The cloth is still hot. I tear the cleanest panel of his shirt and bind it over the root I just scraped, so the quill does not take grit when we stand. His hands stay on the stone while I tie it. He lets me. Above us the grit ticks on the containers. A light passes and does not come down the pipe. The heat in him is climbing now that the wing has nowhere to put it. I feel it in the forehead on my stomach. My thigh is still wet from his mouth, and I can count the lights.

"We have to move before that number owns you," I say.

"I know." He does not lift his head yet. "One minute. Then the yard."
