# Chapter 11 // Vram

> **Redoubt Station 14.** A concrete watchpost on the south edge of the ring. Shortwave only. The city cannot hear what happens inside.

***

The graveyard is behind us. The tiller is still empty as we move toward the south gate.

I take my wrist out from under her hand and move to the tiller. The burn comes back whole, jaw to the eyes, before I have a hand on the glass.

She watches the shake, and the bargain goes out of her face. What sits there is the look a street spends on a wounded dog, clipped by a cart and left in the dust. I hate it. I hate it more than the burn. A price I can meet. This look I cannot.

A brush hits the noise. No skin on me. It is there and then it is gone, and my hand locks on the frame.

"Don't look at me like that," I say. "Tell me what you did."

"If you can hear their commands, God knows what they can hear from you. If this does not stay quiet, I end up in a lab sample room."

"I get that, but what the hell did you just do?"

"Shut up," she says. "I am trying something."

Her face goes still. The noise steps down, further than the brush, and the burn stays under it.

"I think I can feel the leash if you are close enough," she says. "Picturing it in my mind makes me feel something close to what I feel when I touch you."

"Nice. More unanswered questions."

She hears it. The thin leaves, and the burn comes back whole. I look at her the way a man looks at water he is not allowed to drink.

"From now on, you ask. Nicely."

"Bring it back."

She does. The noise steps down again, with the burn still under it. A loud leash is a lab with a drain in the floor, and she will not buy that room with my manners.

The thin holds. I take the tiller on it. The burn sits under the noise and leaves the wheel alone. The palm is for a crisis. This road is not one. She stays on the bench. Cuff still on.

The glass says the south gate. The beacon under it reports to the Dome. I kill the beacon and point the prowler south, at a post the city does not listen to. The route cancels. Manual from here. She watches it die. The click is in the cabin with her.

Command will ask why the beacon died. A storm is an answer. A commander who changed a route is a question I am not ready to file. The lie can wait. A scope on a ridge would need luck. I drive.

"Try not to look grateful. It ruins the abduction."

*She knows.* I keep my eyes on the ruts. The lee thins as the road drops south. The air in the ring was dirty and breathable. Out here it scratches, the first taste of the country the dome decided not to keep. She has lived in worse. I do not explain the road to a woman who can taste it. Seven miles from the mast, a concrete box on the south edge, shortwave only. Redoubt Station 14. If I take her into the city they open her on a table and they open me for not calling the shack. The post is a delay. I am good at calling a delay an order.

I look at her once. Sage, faint, the same as the shack. The cabin is small enough to hold it.

The tunic leaves the shoulders bare. Slate scales on the outer shoulder, small, pebbled, catching the cabin lamp and giving it back dull. I want to know if the cool is only in the palm, or if the shoulder would be cool too. She sees me see it. Her mouth parts, a refusal, not a smile, and the pulse kicks in my throat for a reason that has nothing to do with the road.

*Don't, you asshole.*

The want is specific and humiliating. The shoulder, the mouth, and the thin that is letting me drive. If I reach for her she will take the thin away, or use her teeth, or the cuff. I need the aim more than she needs me alive. I would deserve it. My hands lock on the wheel. I put my eyes back on the ruts and I leave them there. The look stays in the hands anyway. They know where her shoulder is. I do not let them go there.

"You're staring," she says.

"Just once."

"Make it the only once. You don't ask what I am. That was the deal."

The lamp is still on the scales. The name stays behind my teeth. The first look is already in the pulse, and a second would be me deciding. *Once. Mean it.* She lets the noise rise a fraction and gives it back. The burn tastes the gap. I hate the gratitude almost as much as I hated the look. "Where are we going."

"A concrete watchpost. Shortwave only, so the Dome can't hear the room. If I do this right, the city still thinks you're in the mirrors."

"And if you do it wrong?"

"Then I drove you to a box with one door, and I am the man in the box with you." I hear how that sounds. The pulse has not climbed down. "You can hate that after we're inside. Hating it on the road gets us both a retrieval."

"If the box has a watchman, your quiet becomes his report."

"The post is usually empty. There's a shortwave and a cot, and a man comes through once a day. He doesn't sleep there."

"Usually." She tastes the word and does not return it. "If usually is wrong, I want the cuff off before the door, not after. A cuff is a story. A woman walking in is a worse story, and I like my odds better in the worse one."

"I'll take it off when we stop, not on the road. If a scope sees the cuff come off, that's a report too."

"I already hate it." The noise holds. She does not touch me to check. "I also hate a table. Today the box wins. Don't get proud of that."

"If someone's inside, you're my prisoner and I do the talking."

"If someone is inside, you are a man who killed his beacon, and I am the witness who heard you do it. Your story gets us opened. Let me have the mouth if the room is occupied."

"You don't know the post."

"I know a thin lie." She lets the noise rise a hair, enough to remind me she can drop it. "Drive. We'll see if usually was worth saying."

She is counting the cabin while she says it. The hatch, the wheel, the cuff, the door. I know the count because I would be making it. *She's still working.* The counting sits in the same place as the shoulder. I put both with the ruts and I drive.

A thanks almost gets out of me. The quiet is that good. I swallow it. She hears the swallow.

"Don't," she says.

"I didn't say it."

"You were going to thank me. A thanks makes this a favor, and a favor is how a person gets owned. Drive."

I drive. The scratch of the lee is in the window seal. The folded wing takes what room the bench leaves. A feather edge ticks the back of her arm when the prowler yaws. She keeps still. A flinch would break the picture she is holding. I keep both hands on the wheel so they cannot get ambitious. Her breath is the loudest thing in the cabin. I match mine to it, notice that I matched it, and give the noticing to the road.

The road lamp thins. She holds the quiet through what is left of it and does not spend a word.

The mast shows before the wall does. A short whip. No light on it.

"Dead?" she says.

"It's shortwave. The city can't hear it, but anyone close enough can."

"Then we don't talk on the step."

"We don't talk until I know the room is empty."

The post comes up as a low wall and a mast too short to be proud. I kill the prowler's lamps before the last rise. The thin holds until the wheels stop. I want the palm for one more second, and I do not ask. This is not the crisis.

The cuff has to come off or she cannot walk a stair. I key the cuff. The metal lets go. She stands, and the thin breaks with the standing. The fever comes up into the jaw, a tick I cannot hide. She sees it. *Walk. Before you ask for the hand.* I get the door open anyway.

"Walk inside. I'm not carrying you where a scope can write it down."

The cold hits the open door and takes the cabin heat off my face. She steps down without taking the arm I almost offer. The jaw ticks where she can see it. She prices the tick and leaves it alone.

"The hand stays off until we're in," she says. "If I touch you on the step, anyone watching learns the trick."

"The lamps are off. There's nothing on the step to see us."

"You killed them because you weren't sure." She walks. I follow, the fever climbing, my mouth shut.
