Chapter 19 // Tsunari

The radar tick is wrong. The post's own set, the one that listens for company traffic whether he wants it or not.

"Down," he says. "Unannounced. South road. You do not exist in this room."

The vent is a crawl. I go in on my forearms, slate hugged to the ribs, the hook handles knocking once until I pin them with my wrists. Hot metal. Grit in the teeth. My shoulders fit because I make them fit. Behind me he seats the grate, boots on the concrete, his voice level and talking to nobody yet.

I find the relay junction by the heat of it. The bodkin's tang seats in a service jack Drew would recognize. The slate wakes, face toward me, blue tell covered by my glove. No broadcast. A copy, if I am stupid enough to take one. I am here to see who is looking.

Lines fill the glass, a telemetry audit of his post, his fever numbers from the hour he stood six feet off me. A fever, a flat place, the fever back. I know the flat place. I will not give it a name in a shaft. Under the packet, the hand that filed it.

Analyst: Tech-Specialist S. Thorne. Culture Lab 4.

The shaft goes small. I know the initial. I cut the rest of her name off an open set two nights ago and it has been living in my mouth ever since. She is reading him. She flagged the minute. A careful girl does that with a number that looks like a fault, and the flag walks back down this road with soldiers.

I kill the slate. I do not take the file. A file on my chest is a thing they can find if they open the vent. The signature is already in me, which is worse.

Above, a shadow crosses the slit of sky at the end of the duct. Wings, narrower than his, a patrol span catching the searchlight once and gone. They are hawks, early, or I am late.

"Clear the pad," a voice says, not his. Company. Close.

I stay. His boots are still on the concrete over my shoulder. Then a second weight hits the intake, heavier than a man who means to talk, and the breath in the tube changes. Nose first. He has me.

The shaft is one body wide. I am that body until he decides he is too. I hear the breath catch when the scent finishes its work. The jaw is the threat. I kick off the wall, membranes wiping the grit once, and the leap puts me above his shoulders before the teeth arrive. The hook's beak finds the seam under his ear, not the socket, the strap. He slams me into the metal anyway, strength and endurance, and the shaft rings.

"Here," he shouts back, voice thick. "She's here."

A charge answers him from the grate, the woman, and I don't see her. I hear the horn hit the frame and stick. Somebody else is in the hinge, holding it open and shut at once, a wedge with a body. The charge dies in the geometry. Plate scrapes. She cannot get the shoulders through.

The wolf surges. I put the bodkin at the soft of his throat and he twists so the hide of his collar takes it. The hide I need is above us.

I drop under his reach, boots on the duct wall, and I go for the grate at the angle the wedge did not close. The woman is jammed at the shoulders, neck plate and back plate, the chest below the plate skin and heaving, the eyes above the horn bare. The hook skips off the hide with a sound like a bad coin. I do not try the hide again. I rake the beak across the gap at the collarbone, shallow, enough to make her choose her hands.

She chooses wrong. The wedge slips. I am past her hip and into the mechanical room before the horn comes free.

I am only fast in a tube and small where they are wide. They are heavier in the doorway, and the doorway is behind me now, and the wolf is still in the shaft with my scent in his mouth.

Move. I move, because the wing I came here with is still upstairs trying to be a commander, and the blood I just drew on a government soldier has already ended that.
