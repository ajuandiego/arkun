CHAPTER 20

VRAM
Desertion. Draw blood on your own government's retrieval, and you are deserted the same hour. They can turn the leash up on the wing you left.
❖   ❖   ❖

The observer keeps its height on the horizon. Nothing holds it. What lands is a prowler, government gray, kicking grit across the pad.

Three of them step out in Directorate kit, sockets fitted and ordinary in the necks. The heat on the pad is mine.

The wolf is first. Narrow, nose already working, ears tucked to the wind off the post. Behind him, two rhinos. Hide on the neck and the shoulders, a horn each, short legs built to arrive once and hard. One of them is a woman. Her eyes are on the intake. The man has already set his shoulder toward the door.

"Commander Tyage," the wolf says. "Audit and a search. Open the post."

"The runner is dead. I reported the weather."

"We aren't here for the weather." His head turns a fraction. The nose does the rest. "There is a second body in this building. Under the floor. Recent. Fear and copper and a woman."

The leash climbs because she is under the floor and my wrist is empty. Heat stacks behind the eyes. The nose has her. If I give them the hatch they will open her on the pump-room floor, and a body does not need a mouth after that. I keep my hands open, the wing half-spread, so they see the argument and not the measure. They do not touch her. The order does not get that sentence.

"You have an audit," I say. "You do not have a warrant to gut my post because a nose is bored."

The woman rhino smiles with no humor in it. "We have a retrieval. Telemetry, and whatever made your numbers lie. Step off the intake, Commander, or I stop asking."

I do not step off. The wolf is already at the lip, and the woman rolls the shoulder under the plate, and the leash keeps climbing. The moment I draw their blood I am deserted, and the wing I left in the sky can be turned up until it cooks.

"Stand down," I say.

The wolf does not. He has the scent. He goes down the intake.

The man starts for the door. I leave the lip. I meet him short of the latch and put my shoulder into his chest before his brow can find me. He goes into the wall. He stays down. The door does not open. Behind me the woman has the mouth I left, and I hear her in the throat.

I hear the cellar a breath later. The metal rings. A man's voice, thick, telling the pad he has her. The woman is down there with him. The wing I left in the sky can cook, and I can still hear them having her.

The talking part of the hour ends.

I open the door. The man does not get up. The hatch is where I left it, and I drop through it on the wing. The room takes the feathers badly. She is against the far wall, and she is up. The wolf is between us, already in the room, bleeding at the strap, nose full of her, and he is the one still on his feet. He comes for her. I meet him with the heat in my hands. He goes down on the knee and stays, one hand on the cut strap. A grab from there will not move the wing.

The woman is in the room. Plate scored, chest bare where the hide stops, blood already at the collarbone. She comes in on the short charge. I leave the floor before her brow gets there. The wing catches a beam and throws me sideways, and my hands find the gap her plate does not cover, under the arms, where the hide becomes skin. Heat goes into the grip. The vane along my forearm, the one that lifts when the beat is hard, lays a line across the unplated chest. She bellows and goes down. She has no second step.

She has not left the wall. Hooks low, breathing hard, blood on the shoulder and the tunic stuck to it, eyes on me like she is waiting to see what I am now. The hooks are still hers. I needed that before I had a name for the blood.

I know what I am. Their blood is on the pad and on this floor. Directorate kit. My kit, an hour ago. Draw blood on your own retrieval and the desertion is the same hour. They can turn the leash up on Cassian, on Toby, on Rook, on Amari, until the wing I taught them cooks for my choice.

The leash tries. A stitch, then a climb. Her hand finds my wrist without asking, and the climb stops, and that is a second crime stacked on the first.

"You drew on them," she says.

"So did you."

"Then we are both already gone." She does not thank me. "Get us off this floor before the next prowler learns how."

I look at the wolf and the woman on the concrete. The man is on the pad, short of the door, where I put him. Alive, still government. The life that gave me the wing is in the room and on the threshold. I take her hand off my wrist only when I can stand the noise long enough to walk.
