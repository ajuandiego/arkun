Chapter 1

Point of View // Tsunari
Neural Intercept // Audio Log
*Audio Entry 114. Personal Log — Sora Thorne, Junior Synthetics Tech, Eden Dome Alpha.* *"They transferred me to Culture Lab 4 today! The air in the residential quad smells like sweet jasmine and honeysuckle this morning. Director Corvus promised our new aerosol trial will help the people outside breathe clean again. I wish Father and Tsune were here to see this. I know they're looking down from somewhere clean."*

The air inside the maintenance conduit tastes like rusted pennies and dead skin, but it is four tokens cheaper than the street.

I count my breaths by the rhythm of the valve. Clack. Hiss. The rubber gasket on my scavenged rebreather has a hairline split along the lower rim, sealed with three layers of vulcanized tire tape that smells of hot sulfur every time I inhale. It’s an old wound, but it holds. In Sector 09, a seal that holds for ten consecutive minutes is wealth. A seal that fails is three weeks of coughing black phlegm into an oily tin until your alveoli calcify into glass.

They call it The Choke. If you’re lucky, you die of cardiac blowout before your lungs finish turning into stone.

I wedge my shoulder into the ninety-degree bend of the conduit, forty feet above the outer perimeter drainage spillway. Below me, through the rusted floor grating, the Gray Ring stretches into the copper twilight: a jagged, sprawling ocean of stacked shipping containers, corrugated zinc roofs, and makeshift air-scrubber chimneys venting thin plumes of pale steam into the Amber Haze. Beyond the slums, rising like an unbroken mountain range of polished gunmetal, is the Wall.

One hundred meters of solid, seamless titanium-alloy bulkhead. Smooth as a polished mirror, cold as glacial ice, curving away into the yellow dust horizon until it completely encloses the garden of Eden Dome Alpha.

Inside the glass dome, they have honeysuckle. They have unmasked children who run through fields of real Kentucky bluegrass without checking the digital barometric gauge on their wrists. They have real water that falls from calibrated sprinkler arches every afternoon at four o'clock, tasting of mountain minerals instead of recycled industrial gray-water.

Out here, we have the Filter Tax.

I reach down and tap the small brass cylinder clipped to my utility harness. 0.8 Liter-Hours remaining. Forty-eight minutes of filtered oxygen before I have to either pay Madame Chen another scrap-circuit for a recharge or pull the mask off and let the ambient sulfur chew through my throat.

Not today.

I shift my weight, balancing on the narrow steel flange of the conduit. The high, stiff mandarin collar of my tactical tunic digs slightly into the hollow of my throat, but I don't loosen the wrapped linen gaiter underneath. It is suffocating in the sweltering desert draft, but the collar is my life. If a patrol shines a searchlight through the grating and catches the smooth, unblemished line of my neck—if they see the total absence of the twin titanium cervical siphon ports that every collared mutt and factory draftee carries between their vertebrae—I won't even make it to a detention cell.

An un-collared mutant in Sector 09 isn't a citizen. You're an unlicensed biological specimen. An escaped corporate prototype marked for immediate vivisection on the autopsy slab of Culture Lab 2.

My arms, at least, have freedom. The combat tunic is strictly sleeveless, cut close to my ribs from weathered slate-drab ballistic canvas. The cool air of the exhaust duct raises goosebumps along my bare shoulders, brushing over the fine, slate-gray geometric scale patterns that line the underside of my forearms and the curve of my ribs. Under the amber glare of my scavenged welder goggles, the pebbled dermal scales look like delicate tattoo ink rather than hardened keratin. Lower down, heavy leather mechanic’s bracers cover my wrists, cinched with brass buckles that hide the sharp edges of my forearm scutes.

I pull the bodkin knife from my hair.

The high braided warrior bun at the crown of my head loosens slightly, dark raven strands slipping down my neck as the five-inch tapered steel needle comes free in my grip. It’s an antique tool, forged from pre-collapse surgical carbon, ground flat at the tip to fit standard diagnostic terminals.

Before me sits the courier junction: a heavy cast-iron terminal box stenciled with the white double-helix logo of Apex GeneSys.

A week ago, courier runner Ren spotted an Apex courier crawler docked at the south perimeter gate. The drone was leaking encrypted burst transmissions directly into the municipal relay lines—uncompressed data packages traveling from the high orbital Spires down into Dome Alpha’s central biological mainframe.

They thought nobody in Sector 09 had the hardware to tap a hardwired optical conduit. They thought the people living in rusted containers had forgotten how to read machine code.

They forgot that my father built these conduits before they put a bounty on his head.

I slide the flat tip of the bodkin into the junction seam, twisting forty-five degrees until the pneumatic seal gives a sharp pop-click. The front panel drops forward on its hinge. Inside lies a cluster of pulsing glass fiber-optic tubes, glowing with the deep, sickening violet light of Vaelen data-currents.

My throat clicks—a rapid, sub-vocal pulse from my syrinx, reverberating in the hollow of my chest. It’s an involuntary tic my father could never breed out of the Dromaeon splice: whenever my adrenaline spikes, my cursorial brain calculates distances and frequency vectors in rapid sonic bursts.

Three circuits. One optical transceiver. One security tripwire linked to Dome Alpha’s defense grid.

I slide a slim glass optic tap—salvaged from a destroyed surveillance drone three winters back—between the violet fiber clusters. The tap’s suction cup clamps onto the glass core with a faint hum. I unclip my handheld data-slate from my chest rig and jam the lead into the tap.

The small green phosphor screen flickers to life, lines of glowing alphanumeric hex code cascading down the cracked glass like falling water.

DIRECTORATE ROOT ACCESS: OVERRIDE DETECTED
DECRYPTING LAZARUS_KEY_PAYLOAD_V09...
SECURITY LEVEL: ARCHON-OBSIDIAN

My breath catches behind the rebreather. Archon-Obsidian. That’s not a corporate rating. That’s direct extraterrestrial clearance. The Vaelen Spires.

The data stream begins dumping into my slate's solid-state buffer: gigabytes of compressed atmospheric telemetry, gas-fraction models, and soil-phasing charts spanning the entire northern continent. I lean closer, my amber welder goggles magnifying the micro-script.

The state news feeds broadcast over the public holos in the Gray Ring tell us the same story every morning: The Terra-Pylons are healing the sky. The sulfur is a temporary byproduct of atmospheric scrubbing. The Eden Pact protects humanity.

The raw data on my screen tells a slaughterhouse truth.

The atmospheric oxygen level outside the domes isn’t recovering. It’s decaying at 2.4% per calendar quarter—more than twice the rate admitted by the Consortium. But it’s not an accident. The Terra-Pylons aren't scrubbing the air; they are actively pumping industrial volumes of compressed nitrogen-ammonia and sulfurous aerosol catalysts directly into the jet stream.

They aren't cleaning the earth for us. They are terraforming it. Transforming the biosphere into an alien atmosphere that will dissolve human pulmonary tissue in under four minutes without an active filter.

And at the bottom of the telemetry block, highlighted in flashing crimson text, sits the execution clock:

EDEN DOME ALPHA: BIOLOGICAL ISOLATION SCHEDULE
PERIMETER FILTRATION CUTOFF: T-MINUS 14 MONTHS, 02 DAYS
GRAY SECTOR EVACUATION ALLOCATION: 0.00%
DISPOSAL METHOD: PHASED ANOXIA

Fourteen months.

In four hundred and twenty-eight days, Director Corvus isn’t going to open the gates. She isn't going to let the workers inside. Apex GeneSys is going to shut off the municipal scrubbers, seal the titanium bulkheads permanently, and let five million people outside the wall suffocate in their own beds.

A cold numbness washes down my spine, freezing the sweat along my bare shoulders. Fourteen months. Every scavenger, every child in the orphanage pens, every guild boss trading copper for air—they are all dead men walking. We are living in a condemned slaughter pen.

My hands tremble against the data-slate. I initiate the root download, copying the unencrypted mathematical keys. If I can get this file to Gideon Cross at the Glass Vault—if the Bio-Curators can broadcast this telemetry across the underground shortwave relays—we can break the illusion. We can force the Gray Ring to rise before the air is turned off.

COPYING: 78%... 84%... 91%...

A secondary window pops up on the screen, an automated internal personnel directory routing the transmission to its recipient inside Culture Lab 4.

My gaze drifts across the terminal log. And then my heart stops beating.

RECIPIENT QUEUE: CULTURE LAB 4 (SYNTHETICS OVERSIGHT)
PRIMARY ANALYST: THORNE, SORA — TECH-SPECIALIST JUNIOR (ID: ED-8841)
STATUS: ACTIVE / DOMICILE QUAD C-12

The conduit around me seems to vanish. The hum of the exhaust fans, the bitter sulfur smell, the pressure of the rebreather against my jaw—it all dissolves into a deafening roar of blood behind my ears.

Sora.

Ten years. Ten years ago, the corporate tactical teams kicked down the door of our workshop in the lower sumps. Father threw me into the drainage flume while the rifle fire tore through his worktables. When I crawled back out of the black water three days later, the building was charred slag. Father’s crawler was gone, tracked into the boiling mist of the Stygian Ocean. And Sora—ten years old, terrified, her small hand slipping from my fingers in the dark—was listed on the public morgue rolls as deceased during the raid.

I believed she was dead. For a decade, I carved my life out of rust and scavenged wire believing I was the last piece of Jeffrey Thorne left on this poisoned rock.

She isn't dead.

She is inside the dome. Working in Culture Lab 4. Living under the artificial sunlight, breathing sweet jasmine, wearing the crisp white tunic of the very monsters who murdered our family.

She has no idea. The epigraph audio file attached to the directory makes it sickeningly clear: she thinks our father and I died clean deaths. She thinks Director Corvus is an angel of mercy saving humanity. She doesn't know she is analyzing the telemetry that will murder her own sister in fourteen months.

"Sora," I whisper, the syllable cracking against the rubber valve of my rebreather.

A sharp, piercing tone shrieks through the terminal.

CRITICAL ALERT: INTRUSION DETECTED AT SECTOR 09 JUNCTION 4
COUNTERMEASURE: AEROS INTERCEPT VECTOR ALPHA
TERMINAL LOCK INITIATED

"Damn it!"

The junction box slams shut with a pneumatic hiss, severing the optic tap. I yank the data-slate free, jamming it into my chest harness just as the red perimeter strobe lights along the top of the 100-meter wall ignite with a blinding crimson glare.

Sirens tear through the smog—deep, concussive foghorn blasts that vibrate the concrete conduit around my ribs.

Wail. Wail. Wail.

Below the floor grating, the alleys of Sector 09 erupt into chaos. Scavengers scatter like cockroaches, slamming heavy iron shutters over their container stalls. Warning whistles shriek from the rooftops.

I don't look back. I drop the bodkin knife back into the high coil of my warrior bun, jamming it through the woven hair until the steel seats firmly against my skull. I spring toward the conduit exit, my leg muscles coiling with explosive force.

I kick the rusted exhaust louvers open. The toxic desert air hits me like a furnace blast—110 degrees of dry, copper-thick heat that claws at the exposed skin of my shoulders and bare arms.

I vault out onto the narrow maintenance gantry, twenty feet above the sand dunes of the solar mirror graveyard.

I flex my toes inside my boots.

A sharp, silent muscle twitch in my left arch triggers the concealed magnetic release in the sole. Snick. Through the hidden split-ports in the weathered leather, my four-inch curved nanocarbon sickle-claws punch forward, locking into place with a subtle metallic ring.

I hit the rusted gantry rail, my pedal claws gouging deep into the iron, absorbing the shock of the drop without making a sound.

High above me, tearing through the low copper cloud deck, comes a sound that makes every hair on my arms stand straight up.

It isn't the whine of a combustion engine. There are no planes left on Earth.

It is the deafening, aerodynamic whistle of thirty square feet of living feathers cutting through the atmosphere at two hundred miles per hour.

A massive shadow blots out the red searchlights.

I look up through my amber goggles.

Diving vertically from the toxic clouds, his 14-foot feathered wings tucked tightly against his back in a terrifying bullet-dive, is a winged nightmare sheathed in black flight-leather and burnished copper quills. From his shoulders radiates a furnace of heat so intense I can feel the wash of ozone across my face before he even clears the roofline.

Aeros-Legion. The Commander himself.

My pupils contract into razor-sharp horizontal slits behind my lenses.

I bare my teeth behind the mask, leap from the rail into the shadows of the solar mirrors, and run.
