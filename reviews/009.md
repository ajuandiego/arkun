## Verdict

The rooms, the lie, and the fight are already doing their jobs. Chapter 15 reports the weather in full sentences. Chapter 18 spends a whole dome day in paragraphs that move. The trouble is the chopped look that comes back the moment a room, a memory, or a body gets inventoried, and the paired sentence that refuses a label and then hands the real one over. Both are in `canon/style/voice.md` (pillars 6 and 7). The negation half is the search in `.cursor/rules/editorial-redundancy.mdc`. Neither one moves the plot. Both are loud enough, across this stretch, that a reader hears the same tic in the post, the shaft, and the pad.

Spoken orders stay short. "Turn around." "Down." "Confirm." "No body." on the log. Sora's flag, "Anomaly. Repeat. Review.", stays, because the sentence before it says the note is three words. A negation that is the action stays: she does not answer the hurt, he does not call the wing, the wings beat and do not lift.

## Redundancy

### Major. The inventory has no breath

Location: the post in chapter 12 ("The post is one concrete room. A door. A stove that has not been lit." and "Stove poker. A coil of wire."), the count in chapter 14 ("One hundred and six. Seven. Eight."), the memory in chapter 17 ("A ward in the Ring. Sector 09. Rows of cots, a number painted on the beam."), the shaft in chapter 19 ("Strength. Endurance."), the pad in chapter 20 ("Alive. Down. Government."). Same habit, lighter, in chapter 13 ("Night." and "Scales on the shoulder. Skin where a socket should be."), chapter 16 ("Issued." and "Old burns."), chapter 12 again ("Concrete and breath." and "Pride, or the last of the order."), and chapter 17 ("Cold." and "No rail. Profile, goggles up").

What the page does: three or more sentences name pieces of one glance, or a one-word sentence grades the picture. Chapter 14's numbers are the count the voice file uses as the example of the failure.

What the canon file says: `canon/style/voice.md`, pillar 7. Short sentences are allowed. A run of them is not a paragraph. If three sentences in a row each name a piece of the same second, join them or cut the labels. A one-word sentence is something a person says, or a real stop. It is not the narrator grading the picture. A count is the same failure.

Why it matters: chapters 19 and 20 are the fight. The chopped labels land in the middle of the charge and again on the bodies, so the speed reads as a list. Chapter 14's count is the fever the rest of the post is built on, and it arrives as a tally.

Direction: one sentence for the glance. Chapter 14 can be "a hundred and six, then seven, then eight" inside the sentence that already has the tremor. Chapter 20's three words become the sentence that follows them, the life that gave him the wing lying on the floor with the kit. Leave "Anomaly. Repeat. Review." and the radio orders alone.

### Major. The fact arrives by denying the wrong one

Location: chapter 19 ("I am not here to take one. I am here to see who is looking." and "She is not only alive at a bench. She is reading him." and "No stutter. No quiet sent on purpose. I am only fast in a tube"), chapter 16 ("Beautiful is the word I refuse. Built is the word that fits." and "A growth, not a costume."), chapter 14 ("Not to a man's. To a thing I can stand in." and "This lie did not go out on it. It went up the beam"), the Glass Moth ("Not rust. Not a battery's grease. A pinprick of green"), chapter 13 ("It is the sentence she gave me. It is not the post, and it is not the second breath, and it is not the throat"), chapter 20 ("I am no longer a commander having a bad week. I am deserted"), chapter 17 ("Believing them was not required after the first year. Standing was."), chapter 15 ("I make it a plan. *Not a fear.*"), chapter 12 in her mouth ("I'm not your medicine. I'm the woman who can take the noise away").

What the page does: the first sentence refuses a wrong label. The next sentence announces the right one. Chapter 19 does this four times in one shaft. Chapter 20's observer line is the lighter cousin: "The observer keeps its height" already said the fact, and "it does not come down" says it again. "Nothing holds it" is the new piece and can stay.

What the canon file says: `canon/style/voice.md`, pillar 6. Do not introduce a fact by denying a wrong idea and then announcing the right one. Cut the denial. Keep the sentence that says what it is. `.cursor/rules/editorial-redundancy.mdc` names the same tic and says a negation stays only when it is the information, as in "She does not answer."

Why it matters: the shaft and the Corvus lie are the turns of this stretch. Both of them pause to cancel a wrong idea the reader did not have. The Glass Moth's green pinprick is the one wonder in the interlude, and it shows up only after two things it is not.

Direction: keep the second sentence. Cut the denial. "I am here to see who is looking." "Built is the word that fits." "A pinprick of green, pulsing, then gone, then back." "I am deserted, and the wing I left in the sky can be turned up until it cooks." Leave the log's "No body.", the orders, and "We aren't here for the weather." Those are the news, not a warm-up for it.
