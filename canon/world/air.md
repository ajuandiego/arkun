# Air

## 2. Planetary Ecology & Atmosphere

### The Terra-Pylons

A pylon is a biomechanical tower sunk into bedrock, about 2,000 feet tall, pulsing violet as it discharges into the jet stream. Terra-Pylon Seven stands in the barrens south of Sector 09. The towers are the machines that made the present sky. They are not the dome, and they are not a filter.

*   **What the Consortium says they are:** Atmospheric Restoration Towers. The official story is that the pylons ended the Scorching, feed the dome shields, and are slowly cleaning the outdoor haze so the bulkheads can one day open. Sora's "aerosol trial" is this lie in a lab coat. Dome citizens repeat it. It is the reason the towers are allowed to stand in sight of the wall.
*   **What the Ring can see:** The air is worse downwind of a tower than it is under the wall. Glass winds, static, and the sweet spore lanes start near the foot of a pylon. People argue about whether the towers vent dome waste, make weather, or poison the district on purpose. Almost no one outside the Vault knows the gas is a recipe.
*   **What they are actually doing:** Pumping the blend Karyon lungs require: high nitrogen, sulfur, and a methane trace. Earth's ordinary nitrogen-oxygen air is caustic to Karyon pulmonary tissue. The pylons are changing the planet toward that blend, continuously, a little every year. Dome shields deflect the plume. That deflection is the lee. The foot of a tower, and the country far from any shield, take the plume raw.
*   **Effects that are not the Choke:** The copper sky and the violet sunset. The daily silt. The Night Freeze, because the upper air no longer holds the day's heat. Glass winds and acid squalls when a plume dips. Static that kills a radio and leaves glass trees in the sand by morning. Spore orchids, phosphor tendrils, and glass reeds where a tower pulses into the ground. Ionized oceans. The Choke is only the slow human cost of breathing the plume for years.

### The Atmosphere (The Amber Haze)

This section is the rule for air, weather, breath, and the price of a filter. A missing cartridge is not a death sentence. Distance from a dome makes breathing harder. It does not make it sudden.

*   **Sky Quality:** A permanent copper-tinted twilight over unprotected ground. Sunsets run violent violet, magenta, and brass because the aerosols bend the light. Storm-Born people have never seen a blue sky. The sky is wrong. The lungs still work.
*   **Year 1 AS against Year 40 AS:**
    *   *Year 1 (2033), one year after the Storm:* The 140-day tempest is over. The sky is newly copper and the aerosol load is still thin. Outdoor air smells wrong and tires a worker, and a healthy person breathes it all day. The Choke has no name yet. The first Green Domes are not finished (they seal in 3 AS). Filters are a rich person's cough remedy. The pylons are still being praised as the machines that stopped the Scorching.
    *   *Year 40 (2072), the present:* Forty years of injection. The plume is thick enough to taste. The gradient is stable and obvious: clean inside a dome, easiest outdoors in the lee, harder as you leave the wall, hardest in the open barrens and in a pylon's shadow. The Choke is a known slow disease. A filter is a normal, expensive health good. No one under forty has seen blue.
*   **Three bands of air:**
    1.  **Inside the Green Domes.** Piped, scrubbed air. Dry at the back of the mouth, covered with jasmine, gardenia, or cherry blossom. No Choke from the air. If a dome's field dies, that city asphyxiates in hours. That failure is a shield failure, not the outdoor rule.
    2.  **The Lee (the Gray Ring).** The climate field spills past the bulkhead. Streets under the wall are the best outdoor air: hot copper, wet rust, a metallic film on the teeth. A healthy person lives a whole life here with a bare face. Downwind the spill thins, the silt lasts longer, and the same life includes more cough and an earlier Choke. Cloth and goggles come out for the silt hour and for a glass wind, for comfort. They are not keeping anyone alive through the afternoon.
    3.  **The Open (the Rust Barrens, and worse in the shadow of a terra-pylon).** The same sky without the spill. Hardest to breathe, and still not a choke on arrival. Noon is a furnace. Night falls toward -10°F. After dark, sulfur settles in basins and dune hollows: a sore chest by morning, then it fades if you leave. Spore lanes smell sweet and are walked around. People live in cuts, buried stations, and old towns. The open dune is a hard crossing, not an execution.
*   **Oxygen, as a percentage of the breath.** These are mole percentages at the pressure of that ground. Pre-Storm air on the same ground was 20.9% oxygen and about 78% nitrogen. The plateau was already high before the Storm. Do not subtract that height a second time. The pylons have been swapping oxygen out for nitrogen, slowly. They have not made the outdoor air hypoxic enough to drop a person today.
    *   A workday stays ordinary above about 19.5% oxygen. From there down toward 16%, breath shortens, the heart works harder, and a climb hurts. People still walk, work, and sleep. Around 10% to 12%, consciousness fails within minutes. Below about 6%, death is minutes. None of the inhabited bands are in that last range. The Choke is not low oxygen. It is years of silica dust and sulfur dioxide on top of a mildly thinner mix. Sulfur dioxide is a trace, measured in parts per million. A few ppm burn the throat. Tens of ppm are an emergency. The haze is the first, not the second.
    *   *Pre-Storm air, and the Verdant Cradle:* 20.9% oxygen. Essentially no sulfur dioxide.
    *   *Year 1 AS, outdoors:* 20.4% to 20.7% oxygen. Sulfur dioxide under 0.3 ppm. The sky smelled wrong. The oxygen change was too small to matter.
    *   *Green Dome, Year 40:* 20.9% oxygen, sulfur dioxide scrubbed below 0.01 ppm. A private room may be held at 22%. A living space near 30% is a fire, not a luxury, and the domes do not run one.
    *   *Lee, under the wall:* 19.8% to 20.4% oxygen. Sulfur dioxide 0.1 to 0.3 ppm. Above the 19.5% line. A whole life.
    *   *Lee, downwind:* 19.0% to 19.6% oxygen. Sulfur dioxide 0.5 to 1.5 ppm. Noticeable on a climb. The earlier Choke here is the dust and the sulfur, not the missing oxygen.
    *   *Barrens, open ground:* 17.5% to 18.5% oxygen. Sulfur dioxide 1 to 3 ppm. Nitrogen has risen to about 80% to 82%. Harder breathing, the way a high city feels. A crossing of days. Not the Choke. Methane stays a trace, under 0.5%, far below the 5% where it can burn.
    *   *Pylon shadow:* 16.5% to 17.5% oxygen. Sulfur dioxide 3 to 8 ppm. The hardest air people still cross. A night leaves a sore chest. A season of living there starts the rattle.
    *   *A hollow at dawn:* 16.0% to 16.8% oxygen, sulfur dioxide 5 to 8 ppm, and only in that low pool. Walk uphill and the mix is the country's mix again.
    *   *Inside the discharge, at the vent:* 14% to 15% oxygen, sulfur dioxide 10 to 20 ppm. Not a campsite. A few minutes bring confusion and a burned throat. A person turns around. One breath does not kill them.
    *   *A guild sleep cowl:* the street does not change. Charcoal and zeolite take the sulfur and the silica. A small electrolyzed oxygen dose brings the breath inside the cowl to about 20.5%. That dose is why a night costs a day's wages. Step outside and the percentage is the street's percentage again.
    *   *The 14-month target for the Ring:* drag the lee from about 20% down to the open-air band, 17.5% to 18.5%, and spike the sulfur with it. That is barrens air over a city. It is the theft. It is not the air of Year 40.
*   **Altitude is not the city.** The High Aerie is sub-zero because it sits on the center mast at the crown, 3,000 feet up. The oxygen fraction there matches the sky beside the dome, about 19.5% to 20.5%. The pressure is lower, which is what the breathing rigs are for. They do not mean the Ring below is unbreathable.
*   **The daily clock (ground level):**
    *   *Dawn:* The clearest air of the day. In the Ring: wet rust, chicory, cold metal, stills filling, shutters opening. In the barrens: sulfur sits in the hollows. High ground is usable. Low ground smells of rotten egg.
    *   *Morning:* Workable in the lee, with a cleaner spill off the wall and the heat climbing fast. On open silica the furnace is already up: baked iron, nothing green.
    *   *Afternoon silt:* The bad hour in the Ring. Heat, grit in the teeth, cloth over the mouth, goggles. Miserable, then it drops. In the barrens a glass wind is a named storm. The old pylons hum before it pits skin. Full face cover, or get inside.
    *   *Sunset:* Dust settles. Magenta, brass, copper, indigo. In the Ring the bazaar opens: cooked mash, tallow, sage, sweat. In the barrens the same sky is emptier, and animals go quiet when the seeders pulse.
    *   *Night Freeze:* Hard frost. In the Ring it is survivable in a room with a stove and a wall (tallow, cedar, stew). In the barrens an unsheltered sleeper dies, and frost films metal within minutes. The swing runs from about 115°F at the height of the day toward -10°F in the open after dark, because the upper air no longer holds the day's heat.
*   **Named behaviors:**
    *   *The Lee:* Closer to the wall is kinder. Downwind streets cough.
    *   *Silt hour:* Daily and predictable, like a tide. Cloth and goggles, then it passes.
    *   *Glass wind:* Rare and directional. Hollow pylons hum before it arrives. It pits skin, etches glass, and shreds untreated cloth over the course of the storm. People go inside. An acid squall is the wet cousin of the same event.
    *   *Night pool:* After dark the heavy air sinks into cellars, sump floors, and dune hollows. One night there: a sore chest by morning, then it fades. Months of sleeping in the same low room, with no filtered rest, move a baseline human along the slow curve. Roofs and high ground stay the kinder beds.
    *   *The violet pulse:* Dusk, when the orbital seeders fire. The color shifts. Animals go quiet. It is a clock.
    *   *Static lift:* Near a pylon, hair rises, radios hiss, and the morning sand has grown glass trees.
*   **What else is scarce:** Distilled water, taken at dawn from condensation stills. A wall and a stove against the Night Freeze. Warning when a glass wind is coming.
*   **How long a baseline human lasts.** The Choke is gradual. Missing a filter does not drop anyone today.
    *   *The lee, under the wall, no purchased filters:* A whole life. People are born here, work, and get old. If the Choke comes, it comes late, often in the sixties, and then it takes years. Life runs about a decade shorter than a dome worker's.
    *   *The lee, downwind, no purchased filters:* Still a life. The rattle often starts in the forties or fifties. Decline after that is slow. A child in this air grows up. Ren's early rattle is the bad case: years of downwind yards and no filtered sleep, and even then the clock is years, not weeks.
    *   *The barrens, no filter, passing through:* Hours feel thick. A few days bring a cough, a headache, and short breath on a climb. A few weeks and the cough sticks. None of that is the Choke. Back in the lee, or on filtered sleep, it clears in days.
    *   *The barrens, no filter, living there:* Several months before the rattle (early Choke). Years of that life before glass lung. Death, when it comes, is the end of that slow course.
    *   *A pylon's shadow:* The hardest air. One night: a sore chest in the morning, gone after you leave. A season of living there unfiltered: early Choke. Still not death that week. The curve is the barrens curve, shifted earlier, not a different disease.
    *   *One glass wind, bare-faced:* Stung eyes, a raw throat, a cough for days. It pits skin. It does not start glass lung.
*   **The Choke (Glass Lung), in stages:**
    1.  *Film and cough.* Reversible. Days to weeks in the open, or years of slag work in the Ring.
    2.  *The rattle.* Early Choke. Many months of open air without filtered rest, or a decade of the worst Ring work. You can still work. Filtered sleep and time in the lee slow it. They do not fully reverse it.
    3.  *Glass lung.* Years after the rattle. Breath shortens. The heart works harder. People live with it for years. Nobody calcifies in a season, and nobody is put out of a house because a cartridge ran out overnight.
*   **Why people buy filtered air:** Quality of life, and years. A filtered night means sleep without the film on the tongue, children who cough less, and food that tastes less like copper. Over a lifetime it pushes the Choke back, often past the point where it ever starts. It is medicine and comfort. It is not the difference between breathing and not breathing today.
*   **What it costs.** Expensive, and within a working life. Prices are in a scrapper's day-wage, at Madame Chen's guild counter. Black-market cartridges cost about half and last about half, and some of them burn the nose.
    *   One certified sleep cartridge, one person, one night: about one day's wages. A steady worker buys it most nights and still eats. A thin week means they skip, breathe the room, and lose a little ground they can afford.
    *   A family room for one night, children in the clean draft: two or three days' wages. Households choose who sleeps in it.
    *   A month of sleep cartridges for one person, paid ahead: about a week's wages.
    *   A shared cabin filter for a barrens crossing of several days: each traveler's share is a day or two of wages. Refusing it means you arrive coughing. You recover in the lee. You do not die on the dune.
*   **Gear:** Bare faces are normal in the Ring. Cloth and goggles are for silt and storms. A purchased cartridge is for sleep, for a child, for an elder, or for a long stay in the open. A linen gaiter that hides a throat is concealment, not a filter. Madame Chen's brass double-canister mask is the best cartridge money can buy, worn because she can afford it and because the mask is her office. It is not a sign that the street is holding its breath.
*   **Chimeric lungs:** Every class handles the haze better than a baseline human. The same stage takes about two to three times as long, longer for a Galvan in the wet. None of them are immune. A chimera who lives for years in a pylon's shadow with no filtered rest can still reach the rattle, later than the neighbors.
    *   *Ceraton:* Plates turn a splash and a piece of shrapnel. The lungs are still mammalian. A tower's foot still reaches them, later than a baseline human.
    *   *Raptor:* Built for dust and dry air. The eye membranes take the silt. **Tsunari does not need a mask.** Her class, plus the Amber Haze antibody culture her father gave her, means years in the barrens bare-faced before she would even reach early Choke. She is not living that life. Cloth in a glass wind is comfort. The gaiter hides her throat.
    *   *Avian:* High-altitude lungs. A Raven's fast turnover keeps stage 1 from settling into the rattle if he gets any clean rest. Vram can campaign in the barrens. He does not wear a street mask. A rig on the High Aerie is for thin air at altitude. A Kite has the lung and not the Raven's repair rate.
    *   *Lycan:* They breathe deep to track scent, and a filter blunts the nose, so they often go bare-faced and trust the class. Kira crosses the barrens that way.
    *   *Galvan:* The best tolerance for a sulfur seep and standing bad air, and the worst in a dry wind if the hide dries. The shock and the gulp both fail on a dried skin.
    *   *Simulacra:* Immune. Alien scrubbers sit in the lung tissue. Mercer carries cartridges that never sulfur-stain, which is the tell.
*   **The coming theft:** In 14 months the Consortium means to collapse Sector 09's lee, push the open band over the city (about 17.5% to 18.5% oxygen, with the sulfur spiked), and seal the domes. That acceleration is the crime on the clock. It is not how the air behaves now. The Karyon colonial mix, the one the pylons are building toward and have not reached, sits near 12% oxygen with a higher methane trace. At that mix a human city fails in hours. Year 40 is not that mix.
